# 1 | General principles of drafting
@topics General principles of drafting; Relevant substantive rules
>> Good drafting says exactly what is intended, once, in plain words, so that it cannot be misunderstood. Every legal document should be accurate, complete, consistent and readable.

## 1.1 What drafting is
- Drafting is the art of putting legal transactions, claims and rules into writing so that they have the intended legal effect. It covers **pleadings** (documents filed in court), **conveyancing** (deeds transferring or creating interests in property), and **legislative drafting** (statutes and rules).

## 1.2 Core principles
1. **Know the facts and the law first.** Interview the client, collect documents, identify the cause of action and the relief, and check limitation, jurisdiction, stamp duty and registration requirements.
2. **Clarity**: use plain words and short sentences. Prefer "before" to "prior to", "if" to "in the event that".
3. **Precision**: state names, dates, amounts, survey numbers and boundaries exactly. Write amounts in figures and words.
4. **Consistency**: use the same word for the same thing throughout. If you define "the Property", always say "the Property".
5. **Brevity without omission**: include every material fact, nothing more.
6. **Logical order**: chronological for facts; general to particular for terms.
7. **Avoid ambiguity**: avoid "and/or", "etc.", double negatives and vague pronouns. Watch the placement of qualifying words.
8. **Use definitions** for repeated or technical terms, and numbered paragraphs for easy reference.
9. **Comply with formalities**: signatures, verification, affidavits, stamp, attestation and registration.

## 1.3 Substantive rules to keep in mind
- **Pleadings**: Order VI (material facts, not evidence or law), Order VII (plaint), Order VIII (written statement), and s.26 CPC (affidavit with plaint).
- **Conveyancing**: the Transfer of Property Act, 1882 (sale, mortgage, lease, gift), the Indian Stamp Act, 1899 (as amended in Andhra Pradesh), and the Registration Act, 1908 (s.17: compulsory registration; s.49: effect of non-registration).
- **Wills**: s.63 of the Indian Succession Act, 1925 (execution and attestation).

TIP: In the viva, examiners often ask why a particular paragraph is in a draft. For every paragraph you write, be able to name the rule that requires it.

## 1.4 Recent judgments (2025–26)

* Verify every citation; AI-fabricated precedents are rejected (2026).
* Drafting covers pleadings, conveyancing and legislation.
* Principles: facts and law first; clarity; precision; consistency; brevity; logical order; no ambiguity; definitions; formalities.
* Know the governing provisions of the CPC, TPA, Stamp Act, Registration Act and Succession Act.

# 2 | Legislative drafting and plain language
@topics Basic principles of legislative drafting; Translating policy objectives into legislative provisions; Drafting primary and subordinate legislation; Plain language techniques for clarity, precision and accessibility
>> Legislative drafting turns a policy decision into enforceable rules. Plain language makes those rules understandable to the citizens and institutions who must follow them.

## 2.1 Stages of legislative drafting (Thornton)
1. **Understanding the proposal**: what problem the policy is meant to solve, and what the existing law is.
2. **Analysis**: consistency with the Constitution (legislative competence, fundamental rights), existing laws, international obligations and practical enforcement.
3. **Design**: choose the structure, the legal mechanisms (prohibitions, licences, duties, rights, offences, institutions) and the enforcement machinery.
4. **Composition**: write the text.
5. **Scrutiny and testing**: check the draft against the policy, test it on hypothetical cases, and revise.

## 2.2 Structure of a Bill
| Part | Content |
|---|---|
| Long title and preamble | Purpose of the Act |
| Short title, extent and commencement | Name, territory, date of coming into force (often by notification) |
| Definitions | Key terms ("means" for exhaustive; "includes" for extensive) |
| Substantive provisions | Rights, duties, prohibitions |
| Administrative provisions | Authorities, powers, procedure |
| Offences and penalties | With mens rea and proportionate punishment |
| Miscellaneous | Protection of action in good faith, power to make rules, power to remove difficulties |
| Repeal and savings | Effect on earlier laws (read with s.6 General Clauses Act) |
| Schedules | Lists, forms, details |

## 2.3 Primary and subordinate legislation
- **Primary legislation**: Acts of Parliament or the State legislature.
- **Subordinate legislation**: rules, regulations, bye-laws and notifications made under an Act. They must stay within the parent Act (not ultra vires), must not delegate essential legislative functions, and are laid before the legislature for scrutiny.
- Drafting rules: cite the enabling section, follow the Act's definitions, and do not create offences or taxes unless authorised.

## 2.4 Plain language techniques
- Write for the reader: identify who must act, and address them directly where possible.
- Use the **active voice** ("The Collector must issue a notice", not "A notice shall be issued").
- Use "must" for duties, "may" for discretions and "must not" for prohibitions. The traditional "shall" is ambiguous and is avoided in modern plain-language drafting (as in the UK, Australia and New Zealand).
- Prefer short sections and sentences (one idea per sentence), lists, examples and explanatory notes; avoid "provided that" chains, Latin, and archaic words ("hereinafter", "aforesaid", "the said").
- Use the same term consistently, put definitions where they are needed, and use tables and formulas for complex calculations.
- Test the draft with users where possible.

DRAFT: Plain-language redraft of a clause
Traditional:
"Provided that where any person who is the owner of a vehicle
fails to produce the said certificate within the period aforesaid,
he shall be liable to a penalty which may extend to five thousand rupees."

Plain language:
"(1) The owner of a vehicle must produce the certificate within 15 days.
(2) An owner who does not do so is liable to a penalty of up to ₹5,000."
END

* Stages: understand, analyse, design, compose, test.
* Structure of a Bill: titles, commencement, definitions, substantive, administrative, offences, miscellaneous, repeal and savings, schedules.
* Subordinate legislation must stay within the parent Act.
* Plain language: active voice, "must" and "may", short sentences, consistent terms, no archaic words.

# 3 | Civil pleadings I: the plaint
@topics Plaint
>> The plaint is the first pleading in a civil suit. It must show the court has jurisdiction, state the material facts constituting the cause of action, value the suit, and ask for specific relief.

## 3.1 Contents of a plaint (Order VII rules 1 to 8)
1. Name of the court.
2. Names, descriptions and addresses of the plaintiff and defendant.
3. Where the plaintiff or defendant is a minor or of unsound mind, a statement to that effect.
4. Facts constituting the cause of action and when it arose.
5. Facts showing that the court has jurisdiction (pecuniary and territorial).
6. The relief claimed.
7. Any set-off or relinquishment of part of the claim.
8. A statement of the value of the subject matter for jurisdiction and court fees.
9. Grounds of exemption from limitation, if the suit is filed late.
- Followed by the **verification** (Order VI rule 15), an **affidavit** in support, and a **list of documents** (Order VII rule 14).
- Court fee is paid under the **Andhra Pradesh Court Fees and Suits Valuation Act, 1956**.

## 3.2 Drafting tips
- Paragraph 1 describes the plaintiff; paragraph 2 the defendant(s), with the address for service.
- State facts in chronological order, in separate numbered paragraphs.
- Plead fraud, misrepresentation and undue influence with full particulars (Order VI rule 4).
- The prayer must list each relief separately; add "costs" and "such other relief as the Court deems fit".

DRAFT: Plaint in a suit for recovery of money on a promissory note
IN THE COURT OF THE PRINCIPAL SENIOR CIVIL JUDGE, NELLORE

O.S. No. ____ of 2026

**Between:**
[A], son of [B], aged about 45 years, business, residing at
[address], Nellore.                                    ... Plaintiff

**And**
[C], son of [D], aged about 40 years, residing at [address], Nellore.
                                                       ... Defendant

**PLAINT FILED UNDER ORDER VII RULE 1 OF THE CODE OF CIVIL PROCEDURE, 1908**

1. The plaintiff is as described above. The address of the plaintiff for
   service of all notices and processes is that of his counsel,
   [name], Advocate, [address].

2. The defendant is as described above. Summons may be served on him at
   the address given above.

3. On [date], the defendant borrowed ₹5,00,000 (Rupees Five Lakhs only)
   from the plaintiff for his business, agreeing to repay it with
   interest at 12% per annum, and executed a promissory note in favour
   of the plaintiff on the same day at Nellore.

4. Despite repeated demands, the defendant has not paid any amount. The
   plaintiff issued a legal notice dated [date], which the defendant
   received on [date] but did not reply to.

5. The cause of action for the suit arose at Nellore on [date] when the
   promissory note was executed, on [date] when the notice was issued,
   and on all subsequent dates of demand. The suit is within the
   period of limitation of three years from the date of the
   promissory note (Article 35, Limitation Act, 1963).

6. The defendant resides and the cause of action arose within the
   jurisdiction of this Court, which has pecuniary jurisdiction.

7. The suit is valued at ₹[principal + interest to date of suit] for
   the purposes of court fee and jurisdiction, and court fee of
   ₹[amount] is paid under section [ ] of the A.P. Court Fees and Suits
   Valuation Act, 1956.

**PRAYER**
The plaintiff therefore prays that this Court may be pleased to pass a
decree against the defendant:
(a) for ₹[amount], being the principal of ₹5,00,000 with interest at
    12% per annum from [date] to the date of suit;
(b) for future interest at 12% per annum on the principal from the date
    of suit until realisation;
(c) for costs of the suit; and
(d) for such other relief as this Court deems fit in the circumstances.

Nellore,                                              Plaintiff
Date:                                                 Counsel for Plaintiff

**VERIFICATION**
I, [A], the plaintiff above named, state that the facts in paragraphs
1 to 7 are true to my knowledge and belief, and that the contents are
based on the records maintained by me. Verified at Nellore on [date].
                                                      Plaintiff
END

# 4 | Civil pleadings II: written statement, interlocutory application and affidavit
@topics Written statement; Interlocutory application; Affidavit
>> The written statement answers the plaint paragraph by paragraph. Interlocutory applications seek orders during the suit, such as temporary injunctions. Affidavits are sworn statements of fact used as evidence.

## 4.1 Written statement (Order VIII)
- Filed within 30 days of service of summons, extendable up to 90 days (120 days, mandatory, in commercial suits).
- **Specific denial** (rules 3 to 5): deny each allegation specifically; an evasive denial is treated as an admission.
- Plead **new facts** (rule 2): limitation, fraud, payment, release, or that the suit is not maintainable.
- **Preliminary objections** first (jurisdiction, limitation, non-joinder, bar under Order II rule 2, res judicata); then the reply on merits; then **additional pleas**; then any **set-off** (rule 6) or **counter-claim** (rule 6A).

DRAFT: Written statement (outline)
IN THE COURT OF THE PRINCIPAL SENIOR CIVIL JUDGE, NELLORE
O.S. No. 123 of 2026
[A] ... Plaintiff    Versus    [C] ... Defendant

**WRITTEN STATEMENT FILED BY THE DEFENDANT UNDER ORDER VIII RULE 1 CPC**

1. The defendant denies all the allegations in the plaint except those
   specifically admitted below. The plaintiff is put to strict proof
   of every allegation not admitted.

**Preliminary objections**
2. The suit is barred by limitation. The alleged promissory note is
   dated [date], more than three years before the suit was filed.
3. This Court lacks territorial jurisdiction: the defendant resides at
   [place] and no part of the cause of action arose at Nellore.

**Reply on merits**
4. Paragraphs 1 and 2 of the plaint are admitted.
5. The allegations in paragraph 3 are denied. The defendant never
   borrowed ₹5,00,000. The signature on the alleged promissory note is
   not his. Without prejudice to this, if any amount was borrowed, it
   was ₹50,000, which was repaid on [date] in the presence of [witness].
6. The defendant did not receive any notice as alleged in paragraph 4.

**Additional pleas**
7. The suit is filed to harass the defendant because of a dispute over
   [matter].

The defendant prays that the suit be dismissed with exemplary costs.
[Verification and signature]
END

## 4.2 Interlocutory application
- An interlocutory application (I.A.) is filed in a pending suit for an interim order: temporary injunction (Order XXXIX rules 1 and 2), appointment of a commissioner (Order XXVI rule 9), receiver (Order XL), attachment before judgment (Order XXXVIII), amendment of pleadings (Order VI rule 17), or condonation of delay (s.5 Limitation Act).
- It is supported by an affidavit stating the facts; the petition must show the three tests for injunctions: a prima facie case, balance of convenience and irreparable injury.

DRAFT: Interlocutory application for temporary injunction
IN THE COURT OF THE PRINCIPAL JUNIOR CIVIL JUDGE, KAVALI
I.A. No. ____ of 2026
in
O.S. No. ____ of 2026
[Plaintiff] ... Petitioner/Plaintiff
Versus
[Defendant] ... Respondent/Defendant

**PETITION FILED UNDER ORDER XXXIX RULES 1 AND 2 READ WITH SECTION 151 CPC**

For the reasons stated in the accompanying affidavit, the petitioner
prays that this Court may be pleased to grant a temporary injunction
restraining the respondent, his men and agents from interfering with
the petitioner's peaceful possession and enjoyment of the petition
schedule property, pending disposal of the suit, and to pass such
other orders as the Court deems fit.

Kavali,                                       Counsel for Petitioner
Date:
END

## 4.3 Affidavit
- A sworn written statement of facts within the deponent's knowledge (Order XIX). In interlocutory matters, statements of belief may be included if the grounds are stated (rule 3).
- Format: title of the case; "I, [name], son of [ ], aged [ ], residing at [ ], solemnly affirm and state as follows"; numbered paragraphs of facts; the deponent's signature; verification; and the attestation by an Oath Commissioner or Notary.
- Since 2002, pleadings must be accompanied by an affidavit (Order VI rule 15(4)); examination-in-chief is given by affidavit (Order XVIII rule 4).

DRAFT: Affidavit in support of an interlocutory application
IN THE COURT OF THE PRINCIPAL JUNIOR CIVIL JUDGE, KAVALI
I.A. No. ____ of 2026 in O.S. No. ____ of 2026

**AFFIDAVIT OF THE PETITIONER**
I, [name], son of [ ], aged about 50 years, agriculturist, residing at
[village], solemnly affirm and sincerely state as follows:

1. I am the petitioner and the plaintiff in the suit. I know the facts
   of the case.
2. I am the owner in possession of the petition schedule property,
   having purchased it under registered sale deed No. [ ] dated [ ].
3. On [date], the respondent tried to trespass into the property and
   remove the boundary stones, and he threatened to dispossess me.
4. I have a prima facie case, as shown by my title deed and revenue
   records. The balance of convenience is in my favour. If the
   injunction is not granted, I will suffer irreparable loss, as my
   standing crop will be destroyed.
5. I therefore pray that the Court grant a temporary injunction as
   prayed for.

Solemnly affirmed and signed before me
at Kavali on [date].                                   Deponent

Advocate / Notary
END

# 5 | Civil pleadings III: original petition, execution petition, memorandum of appeal, and writ petition
@topics Original petition; Execution petition; Memorandum petition (memorandum of appeal); Petitions under Arts. 226 and 32
>> Not every civil proceeding begins with a plaint. Statutes provide for original petitions (for example, divorce or guardianship); decrees are enforced by execution petitions; appeals are filed by memorandum; and fundamental and legal rights are enforced by writ petitions.

## 5.1 Original petition
- An original petition (O.P.) is filed where a statute provides for proceedings by petition rather than suit, such as petitions for divorce under the Hindu Marriage Act (s.13, before the Family Court), guardianship under the Guardians and Wards Act, succession certificates under the Indian Succession Act, and motor accident claims (M.V.O.P.) under the Motor Vehicles Act, 1988 (s.166).

DRAFT: Original petition for divorce (outline)
IN THE COURT OF THE JUDGE, FAMILY COURT, NELLORE
F.C.O.P. No. ____ of 2026
[Wife] ... Petitioner
Versus
[Husband] ... Respondent

**PETITION FILED UNDER SECTION 13(1)(i-a) OF THE HINDU MARRIAGE ACT, 1955**

1. The petitioner and the respondent are Hindus. Their marriage was
   solemnised on [date] at [place] according to Hindu rites and customs.
2. They lived together at [place]. A child, [name], was born on [date].
3. Since [date], the respondent has treated the petitioner with cruelty:
   [specific incidents with dates].
4. The petitioner has been living separately at [place] since [date].
5. There is no collusion between the parties. The petition is not
   presented with unnecessary delay. No other proceedings are pending
   between the parties regarding the marriage, except [details].
6. This Court has jurisdiction because the petitioner resides at
   Nellore (s.19(iii-a) HMA) and the parties last resided together at
   [place].

PRAYER: Dissolve the marriage by a decree of divorce; grant custody of
the child to the petitioner; award permanent alimony of ₹[ ]; costs.
[Verification and affidavit]
END

- Filed by the decree-holder in the court that passed the decree (or the transferee court). It must be in the tabular form required by Order XXI rule 11(2): number of the suit, names of parties, date of decree, whether any appeal was filed, payments or adjustments, previous applications, amount with interest, costs, against whom execution is sought, and the mode of execution.

## 5.3 Memorandum of appeal (Order XLI)
- The memorandum sets out, concisely and under distinct heads, the **grounds of objection** to the decree, without argument or narrative (Order XLI rule 1). It is accompanied by a certified copy of the judgment and decree (or as permitted, the judgment), and filed within limitation (90 days to the High Court; 30 days to a District Court, Articles 116 and 117).

DRAFT: Memorandum of first appeal (grounds)
IN THE COURT OF THE PRINCIPAL DISTRICT JUDGE, NELLORE
A.S. No. ____ of 2026
(Against the judgment and decree dated [ ] in O.S. No. 123 of 2026 on
the file of the Principal Senior Civil Judge, Nellore)
[C] ... Appellant/Defendant
Versus
[A] ... Respondent/Plaintiff

**MEMORANDUM OF APPEAL UNDER SECTION 96 AND ORDER XLI RULE 1 CPC**

The appellant prefers this appeal on the following, among other, grounds:

1. The judgment and decree of the trial court are contrary to law,
   weight of evidence and probabilities of the case.
2. The trial court erred in holding that the suit was within
   limitation, when the promissory note was dated more than three
   years before the suit.
3. The trial court failed to consider the evidence of DW-2 about the
   repayment on [date].
4. The trial court erred in placing the burden of proving the
   signature on the appellant, when he had denied execution.
5. The appellant craves leave to raise further grounds at the hearing.

PRAYER: Set aside the judgment and decree of the trial court and
dismiss the suit with costs throughout.
Valuation and court fee: ₹[ ] / ₹[ ]
END

## 5.4 Writ petitions under Arts. 226 and 32
- **Art. 32**: in the Supreme Court, for enforcement of fundamental rights only. **Art. 226**: in the High Court (the High Court of Andhra Pradesh at Amaravati, since 1 January 2019), for fundamental rights and "for any other purpose".
- Writs: habeas corpus, mandamus, certiorari, prohibition, quo warranto.
- Format: cause title (with the State represented by its Principal Secretary or the authority); synopsis and list of dates (in the Supreme Court); facts; grounds; interim relief; and prayer. Supported by an affidavit. The petitioner must disclose any earlier proceedings, and should have exhausted alternative remedies (or show why the writ is still appropriate).

DRAFT: Writ petition under Article 226 (outline)
IN THE HIGH COURT OF ANDHRA PRADESH AT AMARAVATI
(Special Original Jurisdiction)
W.P. No. ____ of 2026

[Petitioner], son of [ ], aged [ ], residing at [ ].   ... Petitioner
Versus
1. The State of Andhra Pradesh, represented by its Principal Secretary,
   Revenue Department, Secretariat, Velagapudi, Amaravati.
2. The District Collector, Nellore.
3. The Tahsildar, [Mandal], Nellore District.            ... Respondents

**WRIT PETITION UNDER ARTICLE 226 OF THE CONSTITUTION OF INDIA**

For the reasons stated in the accompanying affidavit, the petitioner
prays that this Court may be pleased to issue a Writ of Mandamus or
any other appropriate writ, order or direction, declaring the action
of the 3rd respondent in refusing to mutate the petitioner's name in
the revenue records for land in Survey No. [ ] of [village], by
proceedings dated [ ], as illegal, arbitrary and violative of
Articles 14 and 300A of the Constitution, and consequently directing
the 3rd respondent to mutate the petitioner's name, and to pass such
other orders as this Court deems fit.

**Interim relief**: Pending disposal of the writ petition, direct the
respondents not to alienate or interfere with the petitioner's
possession of the said land.
[Affidavit with facts and grounds]
END

* Original petitions where statutes require petitions (HMA, GWA, MV Act).
* Execution petitions in the tabular form of Order XXI rule 11.
* Memorandum of appeal: concise grounds, no argument; limitation of 30 or 90 days.
* Writ petitions under Arts. 32 and 226; cause title, facts, grounds, interim relief, prayer, affidavit.

# 6 | Criminal pleadings
@topics Complaints; Criminal miscellaneous petition; Bail application; Memorandum of appeal and revision
>> Criminal pleadings set the criminal process in motion, seek release, or challenge orders. They follow the BNSS, 2023 and must state the facts constituting the offence precisely.

## 6.1 Complaint (s.2(1)(h) and s.223 BNSS [s.200 CrPC])
- A complaint is an allegation made orally or in writing to a Magistrate that some person has committed an offence, with a view to action under the BNSS. It does not include a police report.
- Contents: the court, the complainant's details, the accused's details, the facts constituting the offence with date and place, the provisions violated, the list of witnesses and documents, and the prayer to take cognizance and summon the accused.
- **Section 138 NI Act complaint**: filed within one month of the cause of action (expiry of 15 days from receipt of notice), before the court where the payee's bank branch is situated (s.142).

DRAFT: Complaint under section 138 of the Negotiable Instruments Act
IN THE COURT OF THE JUDICIAL MAGISTRATE OF THE FIRST CLASS, NELLORE
C.C. No. ____ of 2026

[Complainant], son of [ ], aged [ ], residing at [ ].   ... Complainant
Versus
[Accused], son of [ ], aged [ ], residing at [ ].        ... Accused

**COMPLAINT UNDER SECTION 138 READ WITH SECTION 142 OF THE NEGOTIABLE
INSTRUMENTS ACT, 1881 AND SECTION 223 OF THE BHARATIYA NAGARIK
SURAKSHA SANHITA, 2023**

1. The accused borrowed ₹3,00,000 from the complainant on [date] and,
   towards repayment, issued cheque No. [ ] dated [ ] for ₹3,00,000

## 6.2 Criminal miscellaneous petition
- Used for various applications: quashing of proceedings under the High Court's inherent powers (**s.528 BNSS** [s.482 CrPC]), exemption from personal appearance (s.228), return of property (s.497), discharge, and others.
CASE: State of Haryana v Bhajan Lal (1992) :: The Supreme Court listed seven categories in which the High Court may quash an FIR or proceedings under its inherent powers: where the allegations, taken at face value, do not disclose an offence; do not disclose a cognizable offence justifying investigation; are uncontroverted but do not make out a case; disclose only a non-cognizable offence investigated without a Magistrate's order; are absurd and inherently improbable; are barred by law; or are manifestly mala fide. ## 7.2 Stamp duty and registration
- **Stamp duty** under the Indian Stamp Act, 1899 (as applicable in Andhra Pradesh), based on the market value or consideration, whichever is higher. An unstamped or insufficiently stamped instrument is not admissible in evidence until the duty and penalty are paid (s.35), but it is not void (*In re Interplay between Arbitration Agreements under the Arbitration and Conciliation Act 1996 and the Indian Stamp Act 1899*, 2023).
- **Registration** under the Registration Act, 1908: sale of immovable property worth ₹100 or more must be registered (s.54 TPA; s.17 Registration Act). Present the document within four months of execution (s.23). Unregistered documents do not affect the property (s.49), but may be used as evidence of a contract in a suit for specific performance or for collateral purposes.
- Since 2001, an agreement for sale used for part performance under s.53A TPA must be registered (s.17(1A)).

## 7.3 Sale deed (s.54 TPA)
- Sale is a transfer of ownership in exchange for a price paid or promised or part-paid and part-promised.
CASE: Suraj Lamp and Industries Pvt Ltd v State of Haryana (2011) :: The Supreme Court held that immovable property can be legally and lawfully transferred only by a registered deed of conveyance. Transactions by "GPA sales" (a sale agreement, a general power of attorney and a will) do not convey title, and cannot be recognised as completed transfers, though they may be used for specific performance or to show possession under s.53A TPA.

# 8 | Mortgage, lease and gift deeds
@topics Mortgage deeds; Lease deed; Gift deed
>> A mortgage transfers an interest in property as security for a loan; a lease transfers a right to enjoy property for a period for rent; and a gift transfers ownership without consideration and must be accepted during the donor's lifetime.

## 8.1 Mortgage deeds (ss.58 and 59 TPA)
- Kinds (s.58): simple mortgage, mortgage by conditional sale, usufructuary mortgage, English mortgage, mortgage by deposit of title deeds (equitable mortgage, only in notified towns), and anomalous mortgage.
- A mortgage securing ₹100 or more (other than by deposit of title deeds) must be by a registered instrument signed by the mortgagor and attested by at least two witnesses (s.59).
- Key terms: principal, interest, the date of repayment, the mortgaged property, the right of redemption (which cannot be clogged), the mortgagee's power of sale (if any, s.69), covenants on insurance and payment of taxes, and an attestation clause.

# 9 | Promissory note, power of attorney and will
@topics Promissory note; Power of attorney; Will
>> A promissory note is a written promise to pay; a power of attorney authorises an agent; and a will disposes of property on death. Each has its own formalities.

## 9.1 Promissory note (s.4 NI Act)
- An unconditional written undertaking, signed by the maker, to pay a certain sum of money only to, or to the order of, a certain person, or to the bearer.
- Requirements: in writing; an express promise (a mere acknowledgment is not enough); unconditional; signed; a certain sum; a certain payee; and stamped as required by the Stamp Act (an unstamped note cannot be admitted in evidence or validated by penalty, s.35 proviso (a)). It need not be registered.

## 9.2 Power of attorney (Powers of Attorney Act, 1882)
- An instrument by which the principal authorises the agent (attorney) to act on his behalf. **General** power of attorney: for a range of acts. **Special** power of attorney: for a specific act.
- It is usually executed before and authenticated by a Notary, which raises a presumption of due execution (s.85 IEA, carried into the BSA), or registered. A power of attorney authorising sale of immovable property should be registered, and it is often required to be registered before the attorney can present documents for registration (s.32A and s.33 Registration Act).
- It ends on revocation, the principal's death, insanity or insolvency, or completion of the purpose (s.201 Contract Act), unless the agent has an interest in the subject matter (s.202).
- A power of attorney does not transfer title (Suraj Lamp).

## 9.3 Will (Indian Succession Act, 1925)
- A will is a legal declaration of the testator's intention about his property, to take effect after death (s.2(h)). Any person of sound mind who is not a minor may make a will (s.59).
CASE: H. Venkatachala Iyengar v B.N. Thimmajamma (1959) :: The propounder of a will must prove that the testator signed it, was of sound mind and understood its contents, and must remove any suspicious circumstances. Good drafting (a clear declaration of sound mind, a natural disposition, independent witnesses) helps meet these requirements.
- **Execution** (s.63): signed by the testator (or by another in his presence and by his direction), placed so as to show it is meant to give effect to the writing as a will, and **attested by two or more witnesses**, each of whom has seen the testator sign or received his acknowledgment, and has signed in the testator's presence.
- No stamp duty is payable; registration is optional (s.18 Registration Act), but it helps prove the will. Since the omission of s.213 ISA in December 2025, probate is no longer compulsory anywhere in India, but it is still advisable where the will may be disputed.
- Drafting points: a revocation clause, the appointment of an executor, a clear description of the beneficiaries and property, a residuary clause (to cover property not specifically mentioned), a declaration of sound mind and free will, and the attestation clause. A witness who is a beneficiary does not invalidate the will, but the bequest to that witness is void (s.67), so do not use beneficiaries as witnesses.
